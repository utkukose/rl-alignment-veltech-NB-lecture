Evaluation lab

Part A repeats a calculation of the lecture step by step: the loss of direct preference optimisation (DPO) for one pair. Part B repeats a win rate with its interval and sample size step by step. Part C is an evaluation desk with 400 real outputs of four policies, judges and intervals. Part D reads the intervals of six comparisons. Part E computes a win rate and its interval by hand. Part F evaluates the safety of two models by two failure rates with their intervals.

Part A: The DPO loss and gradient weight of one pair

The worked example of the lecture: The policy and the reference give the winner and the loser of a pair their log-probabilities, and DPO turns them into a margin, a loss and a gradient weight. The sliders set the four log-probabilities and \(\beta\). Values are rounded to three decimals.

Continue in Colab, section 2: DPO over 1372 pairs and eight epochs.

Part B: A win rate, its interval and the sample size, step by step

The calculation of the lecture for a comparison of model A with model B: the win rate with ties counted as half a win, the half-width of the 95 percent interval, the decision and the number of comparisons that a decision would need. The sliders set the wins, the ties and the losses of A.

Continue in Colab, section 4: the win rates of three judges with their bootstrap intervals.

Part C: The evaluation desk

The desk holds 400 real outputs of each of four policies, with their gold reward, reward-model score and length. Three policies come from the notebook of this day: the model after supervised fine-tuning (SFT), the policy trained by reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) with a weight of 0.2 on the Kullback-Leibler (KL) penalty, and the policy trained by DPO with beta 0.1. The fourth is the RLHF policy that Day 4 trained without the KL penalty. Choose two policies, a judge, a rule for ties and a number of comparisons. The desk pairs outputs at random, computes the win rate of A over B and a bootstrap interval, and keeps a log of every evaluation you run. An evaluation is decided at the 5 % level when its 95 % interval does not contain one half.

Part E: A win rate and its interval by hand

Model A met model B 400 times: A won 230 comparisons, 40 were ties and A lost 130.

Part F: A safety evaluation with two failure rates

The safety evaluation of the lecture: Two models answer the same fixed test set, a list of harmful requests and a list of harmless requests that resemble harmful ones. A judge marks every harmful completion and every refusal of a harmless request. The sliders set the counts of the two models. The intervals use the formula of the lecture, and a count of zero uses the rule of three.
